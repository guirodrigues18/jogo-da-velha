Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe strategy: 8 tips to win

In regular tic-tac-toe, perfect play always ends in a draw. Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe is different: since every move picks where your opponent plays, you can plan ahead, set traps and turn games around. These tips go from basic to advanced. New to the game? Start with the rules.

  1. Think about the board you send

    Before you move, check where your square will send your opponent. The best square on your mini-board can be terrible if it sends the other player to a board where they're one move from winning.

  2. Never send your opponent where they win

    If your opponent has two in a row on a mini-board, avoid the squares that send them there. A safe, quiet move is often worth more than giving away a board.

  3. The center is worth more

    The middle mini-board is part of 4 lines on the big board (horizontal, vertical and both diagonals), and the corners are part of 3. Winning the center opens more paths to victory. But careful: playing the center square of a mini-board sends your opponent straight to the middle board.

  4. Watch out for free moves

    Sending your opponent to a closed board lets them play anywhere, including exactly where they need to. Only do it when there's no better option.

  5. Sometimes it pays to sacrifice a mini-board

    Letting your opponent win a board that doesn't make a line for them can be a good deal, if in return they're forced to send you to an important board.

  6. Create double threats

    The strongest move creates two threats at once: for example, an almost complete line on your mini-board and a square that sends your opponent to a board where they can't defend. They can only block one.

  7. Force your opponent to play where you want

    If you fill a mini-board with squares that all point to the same place, your opponent gets stuck in a loop. Use it to push them toward boards that are already lost for them.

  8. Use your time well

    Each move has 20 seconds, and online whoever creates the match can pick 15, 10 or even 5. Think during your opponent's turn: look at the board you'll probably play on next. Letting the clock run out hands over your turn for free, and the 4th time you lose the match.

    On short clocks (10s and 5s), don't look for the perfect move: avoid sending your opponent to a board they can win and play fast. A good move in time beats a great one that never happens.

How to practice

Start against the AI on Easy to get the hang of the golden rule, move up to Medium and, when you feel confident, take on Hard, which simulates thousands of games before every move. The daily challenge is great practice too: the position is the same for everyone and you can try as many times as you like.

Then invite a friend to play online through a link and see who learned best.

Strategies for 2v2 Duos

  • Play for your partner: your partner moves right after your opponent. Think about which board the opponent will send them to.
  • Don't undo their plan: if your partner is building 3 in a row on a board, avoid sending the opponent right there.
  • Team up with reactions: a 🔥 after a good move helps your team follow the same idea.

Strategies for Champs

  • Count boards, not lines: whoever reaches 3 mini-boards anywhere wins. Always watch who is closest.
  • Fill contested boards: a full board goes to whoever has the most squares, so taking squares pays off even without a line.
  • Slow down the leader: with 3 or 4 playing, everyone tends to gang up on the leader. Avoid pulling ahead too early.
  • Think about who plays next: your square decides the board for the next player only, not everyone.
